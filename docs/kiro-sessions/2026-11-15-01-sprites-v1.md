## Prompt given to Kiro

````markdown
Create the first pixel-art sprite for each of the 8 Night Shift monsters, as editable source plus generated PNGs.

STYLE REFERENCE
Look at this image for the DRAWING STYLE ONLY (not the character): <local reference folder>/style-reference.jpg
Do NOT copy that image or that character into the repo, and do not trace it. Our monsters are original. The style to match:
- Chunky, low-resolution pixel art; every pixel is a clean square. No anti-aliasing, no gradients, no dithering, no blur.
- A 1px outline in a dark, saturated shade of the body's own colour (for a pink body: dark maroon), never pure black. The outline may be 2px thick along the bottom edge to ground the figure.
- Flat fills with only 2–3 tones per material: a light tone, a base tone, and one shadow band on the right/back side (light comes from the top-left). No pillow shading.
- Soft, rounded, slightly leaning silhouettes with a sense of movement.
- Minimal face: two dark vertical oval eyes and at most a tiny mouth. Cute and a little sad, never gory.
- It must read clearly on a pure black background and on our night colour #12141C.
- Top-down 3/4 view (we see the front and a bit of the top), facing down/toward the camera, idle pose.

THE 8 MONSTERS (read .kiro/specs/monster-ai and src/bugs for context)
Each has one accent colour. Size is the canvas in pixels (transparent background, at least 1px empty margin).
1. Null Ghost — profile.js — 32x32 — accent #7FE3F2 ice blue. A sheet ghost whose middle is EMPTY: a hole through the body (show it as a darker cut-out), body pale ice-blue, partly transparent feel (lighter tones). The word null must not be written; the hole is the idea.
2. Off-by-One — pagination.js — 32x32 — accent #E86FB0 pink. A blocky cube creature that does not sit on the grid: its body is drawn one pixel-row/column offset from its shadow, square eyes of different sizes, one extra tooth sticking out of the mouth, one extra small leg.
3. Infinite Crawler — retry.js — 32x32 — accent #F07C3A orange. A segmented centipede curled into an almost closed ring, chasing its own tail; head with two antennae, small legs. Draw it as a head sprite plus one body-segment sprite as well (the game will chain segments): crawler-head 16x16 and crawler-segment 16x16, plus the 32x32 curled composite for the preview.
4. Async Phantom — payment.js — 24x40 — accent #9C7CF2 lilac. A very tall, thin shadow figure with long arms, dim eyes, and a small loading-spinner halo (a ring of 6–8 dots, some brighter) around the head. Also draw its detached shadow as a separate 32x12 sprite (a flat lilac silhouette with two bright eye dots) — the shadow moves ahead of the body in the game.
5. Memory Leech — cache.js — 32x24 — accent #A9F07E green on a purple body. A fat leech/slug with 2–3 tiny green RAM sticks on its back and a round toothy mouth; leaves a green slime trail (draw a 16x8 trail tile separately).
6. Race Twins — inventory.js — 24x24 each — accent #4FA3F7 electric blue. Two identical small round imps with lightning-shaped antennae and big eyes. twin-a and twin-b are the same drawing; twin-b has a 1px horizontal "jitter" ghost copy behind it (a darker blue duplicate offset by 1px).
7. Deadlock — transfer.js — 32x48 each — accent #9AA6BF steel grey, with a red #FF5A47 keyhole glow. A heavy, hunched armoured guardian with a padlock-shaped chest plate and glowing slit eyes. deadlock-a holds a small padlock with "A" shape hinted (no text needed), deadlock-b is its mirror.
8. Legacy — billing.js + invoice.js + utils.js — 64x64 — final boss. A heap of old beige CRT monitors and cables with a yellow sticky note and a floppy disk; one monitor is the "face" with two eyes on its screen. Muted beige/yellow with a red accent on the cables.

The red file label (e.g. profile.js) is drawn by the game, NOT in the sprite.

HOW TO BUILD IT (keep it editable and reproducible)
- Put the source of every sprite as a text grid (one character per pixel) plus a small colour legend in scripts/sprites/src/<name>.txt (or one .js/.json file per sprite if you prefer). Keep each sprite's palette to at most 8 colours.
- Write scripts/sprites/build-sprites.mjs that reads those sources and writes PNGs. Use only Node built-ins (node:zlib, node:fs) to encode PNG — do not add dependencies.
- Output 1x PNGs to public/sprites/monsters/<name>.png.
- Also output a preview contact sheet to docs/art/monsters-preview.png: all sprites scaled 8x with nearest-neighbour, on a #12141C background, laid out in a grid with ~16px gaps, in monster order. (Labels are optional; if you add them, draw them with a tiny pixel font in the script, no external fonts.)
- Add an npm script "build-sprites": "node scripts/sprites/build-sprites.mjs".
- Run it, then open docs/art/monsters-preview.png and check every sprite yourself: clean outline, no stray pixels, silhouette readable, each monster clearly different from the others. Fix and re-run until they look good.

FINISH
- Run npm run build-sprites, npx vitest run, and npm run build; all must succeed.
- Commit with a clear message (feat: first pixel-art sprites for all 8 monsters). Do not push.
- Print a short summary: the list of files created and any sprite you are not happy with.
````

## Kiro's final summary

All 8 monsters now have first-pass sprites (14 PNGs in total), and I committed them as `a674312` without pushing. `npm run build-sprites`, `npx vitest run` (28 tests) and `npm run build` all pass. I checked the contact sheet by eye over a few passes and fixed the worst problems.

**Files created**
- `scripts/sprites/build-sprites.mjs` reads the grids, writes the PNGs and the preview sheet. It uses Node built-ins only, and it errors on a sprite with more than 8 colours or a character missing from its legend.
- `scripts/sprites/src/*.txt` holds the 14 editable grids, one character per pixel with a colour legend in each file.
- `public/sprites/monsters/` holds the PNGs:
  - `null-ghost`, `off-by-one`, `legacy`
  - `infinite-crawler`, `crawler-head`, `crawler-segment`
  - `async-phantom`, `phantom-shadow`
  - `memory-leech`, `leech-trail`
  - `twin-a`, `twin-b`
  - `deadlock-a`, `deadlock-b`
- `docs/art/monsters-preview.png` is the 8x contact sheet on #12141C, with no labels.
- `package.json` gets the `"build-sprites"` script.

I generated the first grids with a temporary drawing script outside the repo, then deleted it. The `.txt` grids are the only source now, so any later edits are by hand in those files.

**Sprites I'm not happy with**
- **Deadlock:**
  - The padlock in its hand is only 9x7 pixels and its shackle barely shows, so the "A" hint is weak. The chest plate and keyhole read well.
  - The mirror is a plain flip of the same geometry, but I shaded it after flipping, so the light still comes from the top-left.
- **Null Ghost:** the hole is large and sits low, so it can read as an open mouth. A smaller, higher hole or lowered eyes would fix that.
- **Async Phantom:** the arms are short stubs rather than long, and the dark body is only moderately visible on #12141C. The lilac eyes are dim, as asked, but hard to see at 1x.
- **Infinite Crawler:** the legs are single pixels, so the 32x32 ring looks slightly spiky. The `crawler-segment` is rounder than it is segmented and may need a plate line once the game chains it.
- **Memory Leech:** the mouth reads more like a gear than a toothy mouth, and the RAM sticks are small.
- **Race Twins:** the lightning antennae are zig-zags rather than clear bolts. The `twin-b` jitter ghost is subtle, as it's only 1px.

## Owner feedback

Rejected. The sprites were too flat, too small and had little character. The owner gave three new style references and asked for static pixel art in that style. The references are not stored in the repo (licences unknown). In words: a glossy slime with a bright specular highlight; a green creature with a strong dark outline and rim highlights; a detailed eyeball monster. The redraw became sessions 03 and 04. The commit `a674312` stays in history.
